“The Resin Hearth”
In the heart of the city, nestled between glass towers and concrete solitude, lived Lida—a designer of objects not quite furniture, not quite art. Her studio smelled of warm resin and cardamom tea, where light moved gently over shelves lined with strange, glowing forms.
Her newest piece was unlike the others. Inspired by old tales from her grandmother—stories of domovoy, the invisible keepers of warmth and balance—Lida had sculpted an abstract figure: rounded, almost formless, like a curl of smoke trapped in motion. She printed it in translucent white resin, embedded with a hint of rose gold filament that shimmered when the morning sun touched it. It had no eyes, but it seemed to watch over the house.
She called it "Domů"—"Homeward."
On the first night, she placed it on the shelf above her radiator and lit a beeswax candle beside it. The flame flickered oddly, not as if disturbed, but... dancing. The air felt heavier, warmer. Safer.
By the third morning, she noticed the sculpture had shifted position. Just slightly. Its curvature was now tilted as if listening to the living room. The houseplants near it stood taller, the tea kettle seemed to boil faster, and the apartment held a soft scent of pine and bread that she hadn’t cooked.
She laughed to herself, brushing it off as drafts and design quirks. But the Domů kept changing—an arm arc smoothing out, a hollow deepening as though it had taken a breath. And always, always, the warmth remained.
One evening, after a long, tiring day of client calls and pixel-perfect renderings, Lida returned to find the lights gently dimmed, the couch blanket folded just so, and the candle beside Domů already lit.
And in the soft glow of resin and flame, she felt—deep in her bones—what her grandmother once whispered:
"The true spirit of the house doesn’t need to be seen. It only needs to be invited."
